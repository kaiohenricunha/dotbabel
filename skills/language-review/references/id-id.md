# Indonesian (id-ID)

Reference for `id-ID`. Read the rubric first. This file holds what is specific to Indonesian as
written in Indonesia.

## Scope and variants

The target is standard Indonesian (_bahasa baku_), the language of official documents, education,
news, and most product interfaces. Spoken and informal Indonesian (_bahasa sehari-hari_, _bahasa
gaul_) is influenced by Jakarta speech and by regional languages such as Javanese and Sundanese.
Informal forms are right for dialogue and casual brand voices and wrong for formal copy.

Indonesian and Malay (Malaysia, Brunei, Singapore) are related and differ in vocabulary, spelling
conventions, and usage. A Malay form in an Indonesian text is a `regional-fit` finding, often
`major` because a native reader notices at once:

| Malay         | Indonesian                      |
| ------------- | ------------------------------- |
| sila          | silakan                         |
| muat turun    | unduh                           |
| fail          | berkas (or _file_ in tech copy) |
| komputer riba | laptop                          |
| perisian      | perangkat lunak                 |
| percuma       | gratis                          |
| kereta        | mobil                           |
| pejabat       | kantor                          |

Do not assume that a form is Malay because it looks unfamiliar. Check it. Many words are shared,
and some Malay forms are ordinary Indonesian words with another meaning: _percuma_ "in vain",
_kereta_ "carriage, train", _pejabat_ "official". Flag them only when the Malay meaning is intended.

## Register and address

- **Anda** is the standard formal second person in product interfaces, official documents, and
  most customer-facing copy. It is capitalized in formal writing. Keep the capital consistent.
- **Kamu** is informal (peers, younger readers, casual brand voices). **Kalian** is the informal
  plural. **Engkau** is literary or religious. **Kau** appears in song lyrics, literature, and some regional
  speech.
- **Bapak and Ibu** (_Pak, Bu_) address a person respectfully, with or without a name, and are common
  in customer service and business. **Saudara** and **Saudari** are formal and appear in official
  letters. **Kak** is widely used across regions in customer-service and e-commerce copy. **Mas** and **Mbak**
  are Javanese in origin and common in Jakarta. Do not introduce one that the text does not use.
- **Kita and kami.** _Kita_ includes the listener ("we, you and I"). _Kami_ excludes the listener.
  A company speaking to a user says _kami_. _Mari kita mulai_ ("let's begin") uses _kita_. Using
  _kita_ for the company is a common slip.
- **Third person.** _Dia_ and _ia_ are not gender-marked: _ia_ is formal or literary as a subject,
  and _beliau_ is respectful. _Mereka_ is "they". Do not import "he" and "she".
- **Requests.** _Silakan_ invites or permits (_Silakan masuk_). _Mohon_ is a formal request.
  _Harap_ is formal (_Harap tunggu_). _Tolong_ softens a request and is common in speech and friendly copy. Formal
  interface copy usually uses _mohon_ or _silakan_.
  _Silahkan_ is a common nonstandard spelling of _silakan_.
- **Particles and informal words.** _Dong, sih, nih, kok, deh, kan_, and forms such as _gak,
  nggak, udah, banget, gimana_ are colloquial. The formal forms are _tidak, sudah, sangat,
  bagaimana_. A reviewer must not add particles. _Bisa_ is neutral to informal and _dapat_ is
  more formal. Both are fine in most copy.
- **Letters.** _Yth._ (_Yang terhormat_) and _Dengan hormat,_ open a formal letter. _Hormat kami,_
  closes it. _Salam,_ is neutral.

## What reads as native

- **Head first.** The modifier follows the noun and the possessor follows the possessed:
  _akun pengguna_ ("user account"), _pengaturan akun_ ("account settings"), _rumah saya_ ("my
  house"). English order reversed is an error. Avoid _dari_ ("of") inside a noun phrase:
  _pengaturan akun_, not _pengaturan dari akun_.
- **Affixes.** Verbs with _meN-_ assimilate to the root (_tulis → menulis, baca → membaca, kirim →
  mengirim, simpan → menyimpan, pakai → memakai_). The passive is _di-_ (_dikirim_). _Ter-_ marks a
  state or accident (_terkirim, tersimpan_), and is typical of status messages. Nouns use _peN-an_
  and _ke-an_ (_pengaturan, pemberitahuan, kebijakan_). The pair _-kan_ and _-i_ matters:
  _memasukkan_ ("put in") and _memasuki_ ("enter").
- **Passive with a pronoun agent.** The agent comes directly before the bare verb: _berkas yang
  Anda unggah_, _pesan yang saya kirim_. _Yang Anda diunggah_ is an error.
- **Tense is optional.** Indonesian has no tense inflection. _Sudah, telah_ (completed), _sedang_
  (in progress), and _akan_ (future) appear only when time would otherwise be unclear. Marking
  every clause, as English does, reads as translated.
- **Plural.** Often unmarked. Reduplication (_anak-anak_) or a quantifier marks it. A numeral or a
  quantifier replaces reduplication: _tiga anak_, not _tiga anak-anak_. _Para_ marks people
  (_para pengguna_) and is not combined with reduplication.
- **Copula.** _Adalah_ and _merupakan_ link a subject to a noun phrase (_adalah_ also to a clause). They do not precede
  an adjective: _Rumah itu besar_, not _Rumah itu adalah besar_.
- **Prepositions.** _Di_ (place), _ke_ (direction), _dari_ (origin), _kepada_ (to a person), _pada_
  (time or a person), _untuk_ (for). _Di_ and _ke_ as prepositions are written separately
  (_di rumah_). The verb prefix _di-_ is attached (_dikirim_).
- **Collocations.** _berhasil disimpan_ ("saved successfully"), _gagal dikirim_ ("failed to send"),
  _memasukkan kata sandi_, _memeriksa_ (_mengecek_ is neutral to informal), _menginstal_ or _memasang_.
  _Melakukan_ + a noun (_melakukan pengecekan_) is correct and wordy; _memeriksa_ is shorter.

## Transfer errors and calques

From English:

- **Of, with, and has been.** _Akun dari pengguna_ for _akun pengguna_. _Dengan sukses_ for
  _berhasil_. _Telah dibuat_ for _dibuat_ when time is clear. _Pada hari kemarin_ for _kemarin_.
- **"Di mana" as a relative.** _Sistem di mana data disimpan_ follows English "where" (and Dutch).
  Editors often prefer _sistem tempat data disimpan_ or _yang_. `medium` confidence. _Dimana_ written as one word is always nonstandard. Write _di mana_.
- **Double plural and pleonasm.** _Para pengguna-pengguna_, _tiga pengguna-pengguna_, _agar supaya_,
  _adalah merupakan_, _sangat besar sekali_, _para hadirin_ (_hadirin_ is already plural). Remove
  the repeated marker. _Daripada_ for _dari_ (_berasal daripada_) and _yang mana_ as a relative
  follow the same pattern of carried-over structure.
- **Nonstandard spellings** with a standard form in the dictionary (KBBI): _analisa → analisis,
  resiko → risiko, apotik → apotek, sistim → sistem, aktifitas → aktivitas, praktek → praktik,
  ijin → izin, nasehat → nasihat, kwalitas → kualitas, merubah → mengubah, silahkan → silakan_.
  Mark them `mechanics`. The root is _ubah_, so meN- + _ubah_ gives _mengubah_ ("to change"). _Merubah_ comes from
  analogy with _berubah_.
- **Anglicisms with settled alternatives.** _Download, upload, link, file, login, password,
  setting, browser, online_ are very common in interfaces. Alternatives are _unduh, unggah,
  tautan, berkas, masuk, kata sandi, pengaturan, peramban, daring_. Follow the product glossary.
  Do not replace a borrowing the audience uses. An Indonesian affix on an unassimilated foreign
  root takes a hyphen, and the root is italic in formal text (_di-download_). Interface forms such
  as _didownload_ and _mengupdate_ are informal; _diunduh_ and _memperbarui_ are the standard
  ones. Product glossaries decide.
- **"Untuk" + verb overuse**, as in _untuk memulai, silakan..._ when a plain imperative works.

## Orthography, punctuation and typography

- **Numbers.** Thousands point and decimal comma: _1.500.000,50_. The official form of the currency is
  _Rp1.500.000_ (no space after _Rp_). _Rp 1.500.000_ is common. Check the house style and keep one.
- **Dates and times.** _5 Oktober 2026_. Day and month names are capitalized (_Senin, Oktober_). The
  numeric form is day/month/year. The 24-hour clock: the official separator is a point (_14.30_), and the colon (_14:30_) is
  common in interfaces. A zone abbreviation often follows (_WIB, WITA, WIT_), and _pukul_ is used in prose.
- **Capitalization.** Sentence case for sentences and for most interface text. Official spelling
  guidance capitalizes the content words of titles and leaves function words (_dan, di, ke, dari,
  yang, untuk_) lowercase. Follow the product style.
- **Hyphen.** Used in reduplication (_anak-anak_) and before a capitalized root (_se-Indonesia_).
- **Quotation marks.** Double quotes for quotations; single quotes for a gloss and for a quote within a quote. Curly
  or straight follows the house style.
- **Abbreviations.** _dll._ (and so on), _dsb._, _tsb._, _Yth._, _No._

## Leave alone

These are not errors. Mention one only when it clashes with the rest of the text or the house
style forbids it.

- _Bisa_ and _dapat_, both in common use. Formal documents lean to _dapat_.
- Borrowings in product text (_email, login, file, password, online_) when the audience uses them.
- _Kamu_ in a deliberately casual brand voice. _Kak, Mas, Mbak_ in an e-commerce voice.
- _Rp1.000_ against _Rp 1.000_, when consistent. _Berkas_ against _file_, when consistent.
- Informal particles in dialogue and chat copy.
- _Kita_ used inclusively, when the writer and reader act together.
- A passive clause, with or without an agent. The _di-_ passive and the pronoun-agent passive are
  frequent and often the most natural choice. Changing passive to active is not an improvement by
  itself.

## Verify before ruling

State these as questions, with `low` or `medium` confidence.

- _Di mana_ as a relative pronoun, which many editors discourage and some accept.
- _Memengaruhi_ against _mempengaruhi_. The dictionary form drops the _p_. Usage is mixed.
- Title case against sentence case for headings.
- Whether _Anda_ or _kamu_ fits a brand voice, and whether _Bapak/Ibu_ is wanted.
- The current official spelling guideline of the Badan Bahasa (the fifth edition of the _Ejaan yang
  Disempurnakan_, 2022, which replaced PUEBI) and the KBBI daring edition the project follows.
  Verify the edition. Older guides differ in a few forms.
- Regional vocabulary and politeness forms outside the standard (Javanese, Sundanese, and others).
- _Kata sandi_ against _kata kunci_, and _tautan_ against _link_. Product glossaries differ.
