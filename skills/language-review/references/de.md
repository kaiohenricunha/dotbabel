# German (de)

Reference for `de`. Read the rubric first. This file holds what is specific to German, with notes
on `de-DE`, `de-AT`, and `de-CH`.

## Scope and variants

The standard written language is shared, with national differences. Review against the declared
variant. When none is given, assume `de-DE`. Dialects (Bavarian, Alemannic, Swiss German) are
spoken varieties and are not the written standard. Swiss writers use Standard German in formal and published writing; chat and social media use dialect heavily.
Flag a mix of national forms. Do not push one country's norms onto another.

**Loanword gender and plural.** Keep one gender per word. _E-Mail_ is _das_ in Swiss usage and some Austrian and southern usage; elsewhere _die E-Mail_. Others: _der Link, das Passwort, die
App, das Update, das Dashboard, der Server, das Feature, die Cloud, der Cache, das Backup_. Plurals
of loanwords commonly take -s (_die Updates, die E-Mails, die Features_). Not all do (_die
Server_).

## Register and address

- **Sie or du.** _Sie_ (with _Ihnen, Ihr_ capitalized) is the default for customers, public bodies,
  banks, insurers, and B2B writing. _Du_ appears in consumer, startup, gaming, and some retail
  voices. Keep one choice per product. A _du_ string in a _Sie_ app, or the reverse, is a finding.
  Plural informal is _ihr_ and _euch_.
- **Capitalization of du.** Lowercase _du, dein_ is standard. A capital _Du_ in a letter or
  message is older and optional. Keep one.
- **Buttons and instructions.** Buttons use the infinitive (_Speichern_). Instructions use the
  imperative with _Sie_ (_Klicken Sie auf Speichern_) or with _du_ (_Klick auf Speichern_).
- **Politeness.** _Bitte_ in a request is normal and need not be in every line. The subjunctive II
  softens a request (_Könnten Sie..., Wir würden uns freuen..._).
- **Letters and mail.** _Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,_ opens a formal message. _Mit freundlichen
  Grüßen_ closes it. _Viele Grüße_ is semi-formal. _Hallo Frau Müller,_ is common in business e-mail but informal-leaning; _Guten Tag Frau Müller,_ is neutral. Swiss closings use _Grüssen_.
- **Gendern.** _Nutzerinnen und Nutzer_, the colon, the asterisk, the underscore, and the
  Binnen-I are all in use. Participles (_Nutzende_) are another route. The Council for German
  Orthography has not adopted gender special characters inside words into the core of the official
  orthography, but it does not count them as ordinary misspellings, and some schools and public
  bodies forbid them. Check the current position. Organizations decide differently. Follow the house style. Neutral wording is safe.
- **Modal particles** (_doch, ja, mal, eben, halt_) make spoken and friendly copy sound natural.
  Do not add them to a text that is not conversational.

## What reads as native

- **Verb position.** In a declarative main clause the finite verb is second; in imperatives and yes/no questions it is first. In a subordinate clause it is
  last. Separable verbs split in a main clause (_Wir laden die Datei hoch_). English word order
  (_Wir laden hoch die Datei_) is an error.
- **Middle field.** Given information precedes new information. A pronoun precedes a noun. A
  dative noun usually precedes an accusative noun. Time, reason, manner, and place (_wann, warum,
  wie, wo_) is a useful default, not a law.
- **Verbal against nominal style.** Formal German often favors nouns (_Durchführung der
  Überprüfung_). UI, instructions, and friendly copy read better with verbs (_Prüfen Sie..._).
  Judge by genre.
- **Compounds are written solid.** _Benutzerkonto, Datenbankverbindung, Passwortrichtlinie._ A
  space inside a compound (_Benutzer Konto_) is an error under English influence.
- **Hyphens in compounds with foreign or multiword parts.** _E-Mail-Adresse, Open-Source-Lizenz,
  Machine-Learning-Modell, 3-D-Drucker._ Each element is linked.
- **Unwieldy compounds.** A very long invented compound can be split with _von_ or a relative
  clause. A settled term (_Datenschutz-Grundverordnung_) is not a problem.
- **Commas are rule-governed.** A comma precedes _dass_, _weil_, _ob_, and every other
  subordinating conjunction, and sets off relative clauses. English-style comma omission before
  _dass_ is `mechanics`. Commas around infinitive groups are partly optional.
- **Cases and endings.** Adjective endings depend on article, gender, case, and number.
  _Wegen_ takes the genitive in careful writing and the dative in speech.
- **Capitalization.** All nouns are capitalized. Headings capitalize the first word and the nouns,
  not every word.
- **Perfect and preterite.** Speech and southern usage prefer the perfect. Written narration
  prefers the preterite. In UI and messages, the present and the perfect are normal.

## Transfer errors and calques

From English:

- **Sinn machen.** _Das macht Sinn_ is common in speech and is spreading. Careful writing often
  prefers _Das ergibt Sinn_ or _Das ist sinnvoll_. `medium` confidence.
- **False friends.** _Eventuell_ means "possibly", not "eventually" (_schließlich, letztlich_).
  _Aktuell_ means "current", not "actual" (_tatsächlich_). _Bekommen_ is "to receive", not "to
  become" (_werden_). _Kontrollieren_ mainly means "to check, inspect". It also has the senses "to dominate" (_den Markt kontrollieren_) and "to have control over". English "to control a device or process" is usually _steuern_ or _regeln_.
  _Sympathisch_ is "likeable", not "sympathetic" (_mitfühlend_). _Realisieren_ in the sense of
  "to realize" (_erkennen, begreifen_) is now common; the older sense is "to bring about".
- **Anglicized verbs.** _Gecancelt, gecheckt, gedownloadet, deployen, schedulen_ are common in IT
  and workplace speech. In published copy prefer _abgesagt, geprüft, heruntergeladen,
  bereitstellen, ansetzen_. Some terms (_committen_) have no settled German verb. In an
  engineering team, the English verb may be the house term.
- **False friend _Billion_.** German _Billion_ is 10^12; English "billion" is _Milliarde_.
- **Pseudo-anglicisms.** _Handy_, _Beamer_, and _Oldtimer_ are German words with English forms.
  They are fine in German.
- **Possessive apostrophe.** A genitive -s on a name normally takes no apostrophe (_Annas Buch_). Names ending in s, ß, x, or z take one (_Hans' Buch_). An apostrophe in a business name (_Willi's Biomarkt_) is tolerated, not recommended.
- **Splitting a compound or dropping a hyphen** (see above) is a common slip under English influence. So is a missing or wrong linking element (_Zugriffsrechte_, not _Zugriffrechte_).
- **Nominal chains** copied from English noun stacks, as in _Konfigurationsparameteränderung_ or a
  heap of genitives. Reorder or use a verb.
- **Word order and prepositions.** English prepositions chosen for German verbs. _Warten auf_,
  _denken an_, _sich freuen auf_ or _über_ govern specific prepositions.

## Orthography, punctuation and typography

- **Quotation marks.** „…“ (low-high) or »…« in Germany and Austria; «…» in Switzerland. Nested
  quotes use ‚…‘.
- **Numbers.** Decimal comma and thousands point or space (_1.234,56 €_). Switzerland writes
  _1’234.50_. In Germany the currency follows the amount with a no-break space; in Switzerland it precedes it.
- **Dates and times.** _5. Oktober 2026_, _05.10.2026_. The 24-hour clock with _Uhr_ (_14:30 Uhr_).
- **ß and ss.** After a short vowel _ss_ (_dass_, _muss_). After a long vowel or diphthong _ß_
  (_Maß, Straße_), except in Switzerland.
- **Dashes and ranges.** A spaced en dash (_–_) for a break. Ranges use _bis_ or an unspaced en
  dash.
- **Abbreviations** with a no-break space: _z. B., d. h., u. a., ca., bzw._
- **E-Mail** is the standard spelling. _Email_ is nonstandard (and also means enamel).

## Leave alone

These are not errors. Mention one only when it clashes with the rest of the text or the house
style forbids it.

- _Du_ in a consumer or startup voice. A capital _Du_ in a letter.
- _Denglisch_ IT verbs and nouns in an engineering team's own documentation.
- A long, settled compound (_Datenschutz-Grundverordnung_).
- _Wegen_ with the dative in conversational copy. The perfect tense in speech-like copy.
- Starting a sentence with _Und_ or _Aber_.
- Gender-inclusive wording, in either direction, when the house style sets it.
- The comma before an infinitive group where it is optional.
- _Sein_ as the perfect auxiliary of _sitzen, stehen, liegen_ in southern, Austrian, and Swiss usage (_ich bin gesessen_).

## Verify before ruling

State these as questions, with `low` or `medium` confidence.

- Gender-inclusive forms, which vary by organization and are contested.
- _Das macht Sinn_ and _realisieren_ for a given register.
- Austrian and Swiss vocabulary beyond the examples listed here.
- Whether an English verb or noun is the team's agreed term.
- Optional commas (infinitive groups) and hyphenation edge cases. The Duden gives its own
  recommendations on top of the official rules, and they are not always identical.
- Capital _Du_ in a given type of message.
