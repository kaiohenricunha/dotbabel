# Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR)

Reference for `pt-BR`. Read the rubric first. This file holds what is specific to Portuguese as
written in Brazil.

## Scope and variants

This file covers Brazilian Portuguese. European Portuguese (`pt-PT`) and the African varieties
differ in vocabulary, grammar, and typography, and are out of scope. A text that is clearly
European, sent to a Brazilian audience, is a `regional-fit` finding. A text that mixes the two
is a `consistency` finding. Both countries follow the 1990 Orthographic Agreement, and some
spellings still differ: Brazil writes _contato, fato, bebê_ where Portugal writes _contacto,
facto, bebé_. Vocabulary differs too, as the table shows.

## Register and address

- **Você** is the standard second person in Brazilian writing. **Tu** is used in several regions,
  sometimes with third-person verb forms. Do not "correct" regional _tu_ in a brand voice or in
  dialogue. Mixing in one text (_você_ with _teu_, or _tu_ with _seu_) is common in speech. In careful copy, treat it as a `consistency` check, not an error.
- **Formal address.** _O senhor_ and _a senhora_ are very formal. In corporate writing, _você_ with
  courteous wording is normal. _Prezado(a)_ opens a formal letter. _Atenciosamente_ and
  _Cordialmente_ close one. _Abraço_ closes an informal one.
- **Nós versus a gente.** _A gente_ is colloquial. It suits conversational copy. In formal text,
  prefer _nós_ or a first-person plural verb.
- **Spoken reductions** (_pra, tá, né, cê, tô_) belong to speech and to informal writing. They
  suit dialogue and a deliberately casual brand voice. A reviewer must not introduce them.
  _Para_ is safe in every register.
- **Buttons and instructions.** Buttons commonly use the infinitive (_Salvar, Enviar_).
  Instructions use the imperative with _você_ (_Clique em Salvar_, _Digite seu nome_). Keep one
  pattern per product.
- **Gender.** _Todos_ as a generic masculine is standard. _Todas e todos_, _todes_, and neutral
  rewording (_as pessoas usuárias_) are used by some organizations and are contested. Follow the
  house style. Prefer neutral rewording only when the text leaves room.

## What reads as native

- **Subject pronouns.** Brazilian Portuguese keeps overt subjects more than European Portuguese,
  and _você_, _ele_, and _ela_ share one verb form. Drop a pronoun only when it is redundant. Do not
  delete an overt _eu_, _você_, _nós_, or _ele_ to sound native.
- **Possessives without an article.** Brazilian usage usually writes _seu arquivo_, _meu perfil_.
  Portugal usually writes _o seu ficheiro_.
- **Pronoun placement.** Proclisis is common for _me_ in informal copy (_Me diga_, _Me envie_).
  _Se_ and _o/a_ with an imperative stay enclitic in Brazilian interfaces (_Cadastre-se_,
  _Guarde-o_, _Ajude-nos_). Do not "correct" these. Enclisis (_Diga-me_) is not an error either;
  at the start of a sentence in neutral copy it often reads as formal or European.
- **The progressive** is _estar_ + gerund: _está carregando_. Portugal uses _estar a_ + infinitive.
- **Impersonal verbs.** _Haver_ meaning "exist" and _fazer_ in time expressions stay singular:
  _havia muitos problemas_, _faz dois anos_. _Haviam muitos_ and _fazem dois anos_ are common in
  speech and are errors in formal copy.
- **Crase** marks the contraction of the preposition _a_ with the article _a_. Test: swap in a
  masculine noun, and if _ao_ fits, use _à_ (_vou à escola_, _vou ao mercado_). No crase before
  verbs, masculine nouns, or a feminine noun without the article (_a pé, a partir de, vou a Roma_).
- **Collocations.** _fazer uma pergunta, tirar uma dúvida, dar suporte, tomar uma decisão,
  prestar atenção, perder tempo, dar uma olhada_. _Realizar_ and _efetuar_ with a payment are
  correct and formal.
- **Word order** is flexible. Put the new information late. Avoid long chains of _de_.

## Transfer errors and calques

From English:

- **Gerundismo.** _Vou estar enviando_, _vamos estar verificando_ for a single completed action is
  widely discouraged in professional writing, and is often attributed to call-centre style and
  English influence. It is a style judgement, so use `medium` confidence. Leave alone a genuinely
  durative use (_amanhã às 10h estarei viajando_). The simple future or _ir_ + infinitive says
  the same as the flagged form.
- **"O mesmo" as a pronoun** (_o mesmo será enviado_) reads as officialese and can be ambiguous.
- **Passive overuse.** _É realizado por_ where an active verb is shorter.
- **False friends.** _Eventualmente_ means "possibly", not "eventually" (_no fim, por fim_).
  _Atualmente_ means "currently", not "actually" (_na verdade, de fato_). _Pretender_ is "to
  intend", not "to pretend" (_fingir_). _Resumir_ is "to summarize", not "to resume" (_retomar_).
  _Parentes_ are relatives, not parents (_pais_). _Realizar_ meaning "to
  realize" is _perceber_ or _dar-se conta_.
- **Suportar for "to support".** _O app suporta arquivos PDF_ is a widespread technical calque of
  English "supports"; _aceitar_, _ter suporte a_, or _ser compatível com_ are the alternatives. Many
  editors reject it, others accept it, so use `medium` confidence. In the sense of bearing a
  load (_suporta 100 usuários_) it is ordinary Portuguese.
- **Noun chains.** English "user account settings" becomes _configurações da conta do usuário_,
  not a string of _de_.
- **Borrowings.** _App, e-mail, login, link, site, download, feedback_ are normal in technical
  and consumer text. Formal or institutional documents sometimes prefer settled alternatives
  (_aplicativo, correio eletrônico, desempenho_ for _performance_, _baixar_ for _download_,
  _retorno_ for _feedback_). Follow the glossary. Do not replace a borrowing the audience uses.

From European Portuguese, in a Brazilian text: _carregue em_ for "press", _ecrã, ficheiro,
utilizador, descarregar, partilhar, palavra-passe, aceder_, the progressive with _estar a_, _transferir_ for
"download", and _tu_ with second-person verb forms (_tens, podes_) when the rest of the text
is in the _você_ form and no regional voice is intended.

## Orthography, punctuation and typography

- **Numbers.** Decimal comma and thousands point: _1.234,56_. Currency: _R$ 1.234,56_. A percent
  sign is usually written without a space (_10%_); some style guides add one.
- **Dates and times.** _05/10/2026_ is day/month/year. In prose, _5 de outubro de 2026_, with
  the month and the weekday in lowercase. Time: _14h30_ or _14:30_.
- **Capitalization.** Lowercase for months, weekdays, languages, and nationality adjectives.
  Headings and titles use sentence case, not English title case.
- **Quotation marks.** Curly double quotes (“ ”). Guillemets (« ») are the European Portuguese convention.
- **Accents and hyphens.** Check _crase_, the accent on _ônibus, você, também_, and the hyphen in
  compounds under the 1990 Agreement. Mark a clear spelling slip as `mechanics`.
- **Spellings before 2009** (_idéia, vôo, lingüiça, pára_) are `mechanics`.
- **Frequent normative traps.** _Em vez de_ against _ao invés de_ (the second means "the opposite
  of"); _por que, por quê, porque, porquê_; _mal_ and _mau_; _há_ and _a_; _a gente_ and _agente_.
- **Gendered forms with _você_** (_logado/logada, bem-vindo(a), obrigado(a)_) are a common
  interface problem. Neutral rewording (_Sessão iniciada_, _Boas-vindas_) is the usual answer.
  Use `low` confidence; many products keep the masculine default.
- **First of the month** is _1º de maio_.
- **E-mail** with a hyphen is the traditional spelling. _Email_ is common in technology. Keep one.
- **Abbreviations.** _Sr., Sra., Dr., Dra., nº_.

## Leave alone

These are not errors. Mention one only when it clashes with the rest of the text or the house
style forbids it.

- _A gente_ and spoken reductions in a deliberately casual voice. _Tu_ in a regional voice.
- A possessive with the article (_a sua solicitação, o seu arquivo_), which is correct in Brazil.
- Enclisis in formal writing. Proclisis at the start of a sentence in informal copy.
- _Através de_ for "by means of"; some grammarians object, and the usage is widespread.
- _Chegar em_ and _onde_ in place of _em que_ in everyday text.
- Bare infinitives on buttons. _App, e-mail, link, site, download_ in technical and consumer text.
- Inclusive wording, in either direction, when the house style sets it.

## Verify before ruling

State these as questions, with `low` or `medium` confidence.

- How firmly to discourage gerundismo for a given audience.
- Gender-inclusive forms (_todes_, _pessoas usuárias_), which vary by organization.
- Whether _tu_ is expected in a regional or brand voice.
- _Baixar_ against _fazer download_, _deletar_ against _excluir_ or _apagar_, and _cadastro_
  against _registro_. Product glossaries differ.
- _A nível de_ and _no sentido de_ as fillers. Some editors reject them, others accept them.
- ICU plural rules for Portuguese. CLDR counts 0 and 1 as `one` for `pt` (Brazil), so a `one`
  branch can render "0 item". Check the CLDR version the project uses.
- Anything specific to Lusophone Africa or Portugal. It is out of scope here.
